DTMF Broke the Record — But Enrique Iglesias Needed 27 Songs to Build His

Post 03 · Track Record · Dataset: Billboard Hot Latin Songs chart history

Bad Bunny's "DTMF" just spent 71 weeks (and counting, as of early August 2026) at #1 on Billboard's Hot Latin Songs chart — more than any single song has ever managed in the chart's 40-year history. But the all-time record for total time at #1 across an entire career still belongs to someone else: Enrique Iglesias, who got there the slow way, one hit at a time. We charted every song in both records.

The dataset

Two datasets here. First, the "single song" record-progression: the handful of songs that have each held the record for longest #1 reign on Hot Latin Songs at some point since 1986, verified via Billboard's own chart-beat reporting. Second, a much bigger one: all 27 of Enrique Iglesias's #1 hits on the chart, individually, sourced from Wikipedia's "List of Billboard Hot Latin Songs chart achievements and milestones." As a sanity check, we summed all 27 songs' individual week counts and got exactly 189 — matching Wikipedia's independently-stated career total for Iglesias, who is still the all-time leader in cumulative weeks at #1. That match gave us confidence the per-song figures are accurate.

One honest caveat: we also tried to reconstruct Bad Bunny's full catalog of 17 #1 hits the same way, but our reconstructed total didn't match verified reporting (which confirms Iglesias still holds the cumulative record) — so we're only charting his DTMF figure here, not a full per-song breakdown, rather than publish numbers we couldn't verify.

Downloads

post03_longest_reigning_latin_number_ones.csv — the 6-song record progression
post03_enrique_iglesias_number_ones.csv — all 27 of Enrique Iglesias's #1 hits

The method

  1. Identified the songs that have held the "longest-reigning #1" record on Hot Latin Songs at some point since the chart's 1986 debut, and recorded each one's peak weeks-at-#1.
  2. Separately, pulled every one of Enrique Iglesias's 27 #1 hits and their individual weeks-at-#1 totals.
  3. Cross-checked the 27-song list by summing it and comparing to Iglesias's independently-reported career total (189 weeks) — it matched exactly.
  4. Charted both: a small record-progression chart on top, and the full 27-song catalog below it.
catalog = pd.read_csv("post03_enrique_iglesias_number_ones.csv")

DTMF has now out-run Despacito's former 56-week record by 15 weeks, and it's still running — up from 66 weeks back in February 2026. But zoom out to a full career and Iglesias still leads by a wide margin: his 27 #1 hits (topped by "Bailando" at 41 weeks, then "El Perdón" at 30) add up to 189 combined weeks at #1, more than DTMF's single-song record even if you don't count anything else Bad Bunny has ever released. Most of Iglesias's hits didn't linger anywhere near that long individually — 10 of his 27 #1s spent just a single week at the top, and the bulk of his 189 weeks comes from a handful of longer-running hits.

Takeaway: These are two different kinds of records — one song's longest reign vs. a whole career's cumulative time at #1 — and they measure different things. Bad Bunny (17 #1 hits) hasn't caught Iglesias's career total yet, even with DTMF's historic run, though the gap is one to watch given DTMF is still adding weeks. As always: chart methodology has shifted over the decades (streaming weight, radio panels, etc.), so comparing eras is illustrative, not perfectly apples-to-apples.
